Formulas and assumptions

Direct sound from each speaker: SPL = sensitivity + 10·log₁₀(power) − 20·log₁₀(distance). Distance loss follows the inverse-square law (−6 dB per doubling).

Walls are modelled as mirror-image sources. A wall x metres behind the speaker or listener adds a reflection whose path is 2x longer, weakened by the wall’s absorption. In the bass the speaker radiates in all directions and the reflections arrive nearly in phase, so they add as pressure (up to about +6 dB per wall when very close). In the midrange and treble the speaker beams forward, so only the wall behind the listener contributes, and it adds as energy. The bass boost only holds well below the first cancellation dip, at 343 ÷ (4 × x) Hz. Side walls, floor, ceiling and room modes are not included, so real rooms usually measure a few dB louder, especially at longer distances.

Two speakers, power sum: 10·log₁₀(10^(L₁/10) + 10^(L₂/10)). Pressure sum: 20·log₁₀(10^(L₁/20) + 10^(L₂/20)). Pressure summing only applies when both play the same signal and arrive in phase, which is realistic at low frequencies or on the centre line for mono content.

Results are continuous (sine-wave-equivalent) levels at full rated amplifier output, ignoring power compression, which can cost 1–3 dB or more near a driver's limits. Music peaks will sit above average levels by the signal's crest factor.
